KALLIROI COMMUNITY TRUST - QUARTERLY FIELD REPORT (SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT)
Reporting period: April to June 2026
Prepared by the programmes team, Athens office

1. Overview

The Kalliroi Community Trust operates three neighbourhood advice centres in the
western districts of the city, providing legal orientation, language support and
emergency food assistance to newly arrived families. This quarter the Trust
supported 412 households, an increase of 31 per cent on the previous quarter.
The increase is driven almost entirely by arrivals from two reception sites that
closed in April, and the programmes team expects demand to stay at this level
through the autumn.

2. Service delivery

The advice centres opened for 214 sessions across the quarter. Legal orientation
remains the most requested service, accounting for 58 per cent of appointments,
followed by language support at 26 per cent. Waiting times for a legal
orientation appointment rose from four days to eleven days in May, before
falling back to six days in June after two volunteer caseworkers completed
training. The food assistance programme distributed 3,120 parcels, of which 40
per cent went to households with children under five.

3. Staffing and volunteers

The Trust employs six part-time staff and works with 34 active volunteers.
Volunteer retention is a persistent problem: 12 volunteers left during the
quarter, most citing the emotional weight of casework and the absence of
structured debriefing. The programmes coordinator, Eleni Papadaki, can be
reached at eleni.papadaki@kalliroi-trust.example for questions about the
volunteer programme, or on +30 210 555 0134. A structured supervision pilot
begins in September.

4. Safeguarding

Two safeguarding concerns were raised during the quarter and both were referred
to the municipal child protection service within 24 hours. Neither concerned a
member of Trust staff. The safeguarding lead has asked that all casework notes
be moved off shared drives and into the case management system by the end of
August. Case reference numbers 20264417 and 20264498 remain open.

5. Funding

The Trust closed the quarter with 4.2 months of unrestricted reserves, below the
six-month target set by the board. Two multi-year grant applications are
outstanding. A third application to a municipal fund was unsuccessful; feedback
indicated the application did not evidence outcomes for beneficiaries clearly
enough. The finance officer notes that the current restricted-to-unrestricted
ratio makes core costs difficult to cover, and recommends the board consider a
reserves policy review.

6. Risks

The programmes team identifies three principal risks. First, the reserves
position leaves no room to absorb a delayed grant payment. Second, dependence on
a small volunteer pool creates single points of failure at two of the three
centres. Third, the closure of the reception sites has shifted demand
geographically, and the eastern district now has no advice provision at all
despite a growing population of recent arrivals.

7. Recommendations

The programmes team recommends that the board approve a reserves policy review
in September, fund a part-time volunteer coordinator post from the September
grant if awarded, and commission a short scoping study on provision in the
eastern district before the end of the year.
